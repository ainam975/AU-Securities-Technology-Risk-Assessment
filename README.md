# A.U. Securities — Technology Risk & Controls Assessment

A.U. Securities is a fictional U.S. online brokerage platform created for a simulated technology risk and controls assessment. The project evaluates technology and operational risks associated with customer authentication, employee access, deposits and withdrawals, trade processing, security monitoring, third-party services, business continuity, data protection, vulnerability management, and related brokerage operations.

## Project Objective

The objective of this assessment was to identify key risks within an online brokerage environment, evaluate the controls designed to address those risks, document control gaps, and develop audit procedures and recommendations.

The project was designed to demonstrate practical experience with technology risk, IT controls, GRC, IT audit concepts, brokerage operations, and Excel-based risk analysis.

## Assessment Approach

Risks were documented in a Risk Register and scored using likelihood and impact to determine inherent risk.

Key controls were then mapped to those risks in a Risk & Control Matrix. Each selected control includes its objective, control type, control nature, frequency, owner, audit procedure, expected evidence, design assessment, and identified control gap.

Significant weaknesses were developed into prioritized audit findings and summarized in an executive risk assessment report.

## Project Deliverables

- Brokerage Process & System Diagram
- Risk Register + Risk & Control Matrix Workbook
- Audit Findings Report
- Executive Risk Assessment Report

## Key Results

The assessment identified 12 inherent risks:

- 2 Critical
- 8 High
- 1 Moderate
- 1 Low

The highest-priority areas included customer account protection, unauthorized money movement, privileged access, security monitoring, disaster recovery testing, and vulnerability remediation.

Five prioritized audit findings were developed. One Critical finding represents two related Critical risks that were consolidated because they share the same underlying step-up authentication weakness.
